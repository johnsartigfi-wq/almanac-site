September 18, 2026: The Unhurried Step

Slowness ✦ Walking Pace ✦ Unhurry ✦ Tempo ✦ The Speed of the Soul

A meal goes by in three bites, a question in half a listen, a child in a glance over a shoulder already turning. At a walk, all three come back. That is walking pace, the tempo the deep things keep: hurry does not quicken them, it only thins them. The next cup you lift is small enough to practice on.

Overall Frequency

Watch a hurried person eat, and you will see food disappear without being tasted. Watch a hurried person listen, and you will see a reply being loaded, not a word being received. Hurry does not merely speed the good life up: it swaps it for another life entirely, thinner, blurrier, strangely joyless at any speed. This morning the traditions gather at walking pace, because every one of them, from every century including the unhurried ones, names hurry as a spiritual problem: not busyness exactly, full days can be deep days, but the inner rush: the leaning forward out of the present hour, the tempo at which nothing can land. The desert monks called it acedia's cousin: the moderns have called it, in a diagnosis that has become the day's modern proverb, the chief adversary of the spiritual life: and the remedy in every lens is the same, and almost embarrassingly available: slow down: not the calendar first: the step.

A Reflection Question

What am I hurrying that hurry cannot speed up, only thin?

A Practice for Today

Walk an hour today at true walking pace, phone away, nowhere you have to be. Take one stuck problem along and see how it looks by the end.

Universal Guidance

✦

Hurry never saved the hour it promised; it only guaranteed you weren't there for this one. Slow to the soul's walking pace, and watch the world reappear: it was there all along, moving at exactly the speed you stopped going.
