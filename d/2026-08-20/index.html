August 20, 2026: The Common Well

The Commons ✦ Contribution ✦ Shared Good ✦ Stewardship ✦ Civic Care

The well was full before you came: rope worn smooth and rim swept clean by hands long gone. Nobody owns it and everybody drinks from it, which is all the commons has ever been. Its law is old: whoever draws helps keep it, and the keeping is small, a leaf cleared, a strand mended. One well sits inside your day, near enough to tend.

Overall Frequency

At the center of every old village stood a well that nobody owned and everybody drank from, and around that well grew an unwritten law older than any government: whoever uses the well helps keep it. Clears the leaves. Mends the rope. Teaches the children not to foul it. This morning the traditions gather at the common well, because the village has grown to the size of the world and the wells have multiplied beyond counting: the roads, the language, the laws, the libraries, the vaccines, the water itself, and the old law has not changed: everyone drinks; someone must tend.

A Reflection Question

What shared thing did I use this morning that I have never helped keep in repair?

A Practice for Today

List the shared things today ran on: water, roads, the library, the free knowledge online, and mark the one nearest you in worst repair.
